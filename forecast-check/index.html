III. What it says

    How it’s scored

    Bias
    The total of forecast minus actual, divided by the total of the actuals. Negative means your forecasts ran low.
    Average miss
    The total of the misses, ignoring direction, divided by the total of the actuals (often called WAPE). Big periods count for more than small ones, so a miss on a tiny month can’t dominate.
    On target
    The share of forecasts within the tolerance you set. Rows with an actual of zero are left out of the percentage measures.
    Order
    Rows are read in the order you paste them, so put the oldest first. “The last 12” means the bottom 12 rows.
    Brier score
    The average of (probability − outcome)², where the outcome is 1 if it happened and 0 if not. Zero is perfect, and always saying 50% scores 0.25.
    Skill
    How much better your Brier score is than always forecasting the overall rate in your own data. Above zero means your forecasts add something.
    Calibration
    Forecasts grouped in 10-point bands. In a well-calibrated set, things you call 70% happen about 70% of the time.
